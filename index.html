Why It Matters

Overview

Problem

In pHRC, optimal performance is achieved when a robot maximizes its assistance toward the human's intended goal. Reaching this level of support requires early intent inference, compliance under uncertainty, rapid adaptation to shifting human actions, and smooth transitions between following and leading behaviors, all without relying on intrusive measurements. Current methods fail to unify all of these capabilities into a single framework.

Core Idea

AIF provides a promising framework that unifies action and inference, inspired by how living agents continuously act to understand their environment and achieve their goals. Although AIF has strong potential for human robot interaction, it remains underexplored in real-world pHRC settings. This thesis uses deep AIF to allow the robot to naturally handle uncertainty while pursuing goal progress and inferring human intent, leading to effective collaboration.

Contribution

The proposed method integrates an ACT candidate-policy model, a learned World Model, and a categorical goal posterior into a real-time deep AIF system. Multiple candidate action chunks are proposed, evaluated, and selected by balancing task progress with information gain about the human's intended goal, while a confidence-aware role-adaptation mechanism ensures smooth transitions between following and leading behaviors. The system is evaluated on a real-world pHRC setup in a collaborative box-handling task.

Abstract

Effective physical human-robot collaboration (pHRC) requires a robot to infer the human's intent early, act under uncertainty, adapt quickly to changing human behavior, and shift smoothly between leading and following, all without intrusive measurements. Existing methods fall short because most are reactive, unable to intentionally lead, assist, or actively try to infer intent when it is unclear, and no approach unifies these capabilities.

Active inference (AIF) offers a promising alternative. Inspired by how living agents act to understand their environment while pursuing goals, AIF selects actions that both advance a task and reduce uncertainty, giving a robot a natural way to infer human intent while adapting its behavior in real time. Despite this potential, AIF remains underexplored in real-world human-robot interaction (HRI). This thesis investigates deep AIF as a framework for handling uncertainty while jointly pursuing goal progress, inferring intent, and adapting behavior for proactive collaboration.

The proposed system pairs an Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy with a learned world model that maintains an explicit categorical posterior over human goals. The ACT-based policy model proposes multiple temporally coherent candidate action chunks from recent proprioceptive observations. The world model then scores these candidates by expected free energy (EFE), balancing information gain about the human's goal and interaction state against the pragmatic value of leading or following.

The approach is evaluated on a Franka Research 3 (FR3) robot in a force-dependent box-lifting and transport task, where the box is held only by the clamping force of both agents and the human secretly selects the target location. Against passive-inference and behavior-cloning baselines, the AIF framework resolves goal uncertainty faster and provides stronger collaborative assistance, though EFE-based selection remains sensitive to world model accuracy. These results show that ACT is an effective policy proposal model for real-time deep AIF, and position AIF as a promising paradigm for unifying intent inference, information-seeking, and proactive assistance in pHRI.

Method

Method

01

Observe

End-effector pose, velocity, wrench, command pose, and goal belief and interaction mode are collected into a temporal observation window.

02

Predict

An ACT-based policy model proposes temporally coherent relative action chunks. A World Model predicts future observations under those candidate policies.

03

Select

EFE combines pragmatic value (goal directed behavior), goal information gain, and state information gain. 40 candidate action chunks are generated, evaluated, and the one with the lowest EFE is selected.

04

Act and Update

The Cartesian impedance controller executes the selected chunk while the World Model updates from new observations.

Execution

The execution layer builds the recent interaction history, sends it to a GPU server, receives the selected action chunk, and turns it into smooth robot motion. The server proposes 40 candidate action chunks, evaluates them with the World Model and EFE, and returns the action expected to best balance task progress and uncertainty reduction.

  • 30 Hz observation and update loop with 10-step planning phases.
  • Predictions are made in advance and precomputed so selected actions arrive at the robot on time without delay.
  • Startup and handoff logic moves the robot into the box-grab pose and returns it to a usable pose after success, timeout, or stacking transitions.
  • Handles stale predictions, workspace limits, and trial-end behavior.

Quantitative results

The proposed AIF system is compared against passive-value selection without information gain and a policy model baseline that infers the goal posterior but does not actively plan for uncertainty reduction. For each method, 10 trials per goal were conducted, resulting in a total of 30 trials for each method.

Ablation

Reduced Planning Frequency with an Adaptive Cascaded Controller

  • Reduced Planning Frequency The planning frequency was reduced from 3 Hz to 1 Hz so the robot could execute a larger part of each trajectory, enabling more distinct exploration actions.
  • Cascaded Admittance-Impedance Control A Cartesian admittance controller was inserted before the impedance controller, allowing high-frequency force interaction to be handled locally while the planner selects higher-level actions.
  • Adaptive Stiffness Control The admittance stiffness was adjusted based on interaction mode, allowing the robot to follow human forces more when following and assist more strongly when leading.

Conclusion

Conclusion

Findings

The full AIF system reduced uncertainty about the human's intended goal more quickly than passive inference and assisted the human more effectively than non-planning policy baselines. Combined with a natural-feeling transition between following and leading, this resulted in superior collaboration performance. The policy model successfully generated diverse and coherent action chunks within the online timing budget.

Limitations and Future Work

The benefit of EFE-based selection depends on World Model accuracy, and the experiments were limited to a controlled task with fixed goals and one human operator. Future work should improve dataset coverage, reduce rollout noise, evaluate multiple participants, add visual scene conditioning, study action-chunk length, investigate delta actions together with a low-level admittance controller, and replace threshold-based mode switches with continuous coupling through goal entropy and surprise.
